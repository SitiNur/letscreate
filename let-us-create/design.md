# Let Us Create — Visual Direction

## Creative Objective

Let Us Create should belong to the same family as Let Us Design without looking like a children's reskin of the studio. The family resemblance should come from shared creative confidence, rounded forms, expressive composition, bold color accents, and human language.

The distinction:

- **Let Us Design:** sophisticated, dark, dramatic, portfolio-led.
- **Let Us Create:** bright, open, tactile, participatory, learning-led.
- **Let Us Build:** can later become more systematic, functional, and product-led.

## Shared Family DNA

Retain or reinterpret these elements from Let Us Design:

- The custom `CoolveticaRound by LetUs` typeface as a recognizable display voice.
- Black and warm white as grounding neutrals.
- Bold purple, yellow, green, pink, and orange accents.
- Rounded geometry and playful oversized typography.
- Grid-based composition, but make the grid resemble a creative workspace.
- Hand-drawn annotations as a human counterpoint to digital precision.
- Motion that reveals a process: sketch → test → create.

Avoid copying the dark vignette, dramatic spotlight, and dense portfolio aesthetic directly. Let Us Create needs more daylight, breathing room, and visible participation.

## Direction A — Creative Playground

### Concept

A joyful digital playground built from modular shapes. Children can rearrange, connect, and transform visual blocks just as they do when coding or designing.

**Best for:** the clearest family connection and a broad coding + design platform.  
**Mood:** energetic, optimistic, imaginative, contemporary.  
**Recommendation:** strongest overall direction.

### Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | Almost Black | `#121212` |
| Canvas | Soft Cream | `#FFF9EC` |
| Primary | Create Purple | `#7C4DFF` |
| Spark | Sunny Yellow | `#FFD84D` |
| Growth | Lime Green | `#B9F45D` |
| Imagination | Coral Pink | `#FF6685` |
| Digital | Sky Blue | `#55C7FF` |

This palette is related to Let Us Design's purple `#9747FF`, yellow `#F7C608`, green `#B6FE20`, and pink `#FF4D7D`, but softened for a brighter learning environment.

### Typography

- **Display:** CoolveticaRound by LetUs — large headlines and campaign statements.
- **Body/UI:** Plus Jakarta Sans — friendly, legible, and already present in the Let Us Design ecosystem.
- **Accent:** Gloria Hallelujah — only for brief annotations, arrows, or children's quotes.

Use the handwritten font sparingly. Important learning instructions must remain easy to read.

### Image and Illustration Style

- Modular 2D shapes that resemble blocks, stickers, windows, cursors, and pixels.
- Children's creations shown inside playful frames instead of generic decorative mockups.
- Simple characters assembled from geometric parts; slightly imperfect and expressive.
- Real photography of hands making, drawing, arranging cards, or presenting a project.
- Hand-drawn arrows and notes connecting an initial idea to the final output.

### Signature Device

**The Creation Trail:** a dotted or hand-drawn line traveling through `Imagine → Explore → Create`, changing form as it moves. It can become a page divider, animation path, certificate element, or social-media motif.

### Sample Art Direction

> A warm cream canvas filled with colorful modular blocks, a child's hand moving one purple code block, playful geometric characters, hand-drawn arrows, visible paper texture, clean editorial composition, bright natural light, no stock-photo classroom aesthetic.

## Direction B — Idea Notebook

### Concept

A living sketchbook where early ideas, experiments, notes, and finished creations are equally valuable. The identity makes the learning process visible rather than presenting only polished outcomes.

**Best for:** emphasizing creativity, facilitation, and the expansion into design classes.  
**Mood:** thoughtful, warm, handmade, curious.  
**Risk:** can appear like an art class unless digital cues are consistently included.

### Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Pencil | Charcoal | `#242424` |
| Paper | Warm Paper | `#FFFDF4` |
| Primary | Ink Blue | `#4355D9` |
| Marker | Tangerine | `#FF7A45` |
| Highlighter | Lemon | `#F6E85D` |
| Note | Lavender | `#C9B8FF` |
| Success | Mint | `#A8E6CF` |

### Typography

- **Display:** CoolveticaRound by LetUs.
- **Body:** Atkinson Hyperlegible or Nunito Sans for excellent readability.
- **Annotation:** Gloria Hallelujah for short labels only.

If avoiding an additional font dependency, use Plus Jakarta Sans in place of Atkinson Hyperlegible.

### Image and Illustration Style

- Mixed-media collage combining screenshots, crayon marks, sticky notes, grids, and cut paper.
- Before-and-after storytelling: sketch on the left, working prototype on the right.
- Cropped documentary photography focused on process rather than posed smiling portraits.
- Children's words preserved in their own handwriting when permission is available.
- Interface fragments and code blocks treated like scrapbook pieces.

### Signature Device

**Idea Cards:** index-card-like containers carrying a child's question, sketch, experiment, and result. Cards can organize website content and physical classroom materials.

### Sample Art Direction

> An editorial children's idea notebook photographed from above, warm paper, simple interface sketches, colorful sticky notes, one small game screenshot, real pencil marks, playful but organized, premium education brand, generous whitespace.

## Direction C — Friendly Future Lab

### Concept

A creative technology lab that feels futuristic without becoming cold, corporate, or overly STEM-coded. It speaks especially well to institutional partners and older children.

**Best for:** school partnerships, technology positioning, and future programs for ages 9+.  
**Mood:** inventive, capable, bright, forward-looking.  
**Risk:** needs human photography and hand-made details to remain warm.

### Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Ink | Midnight | `#15142B` |
| Canvas | Cloud | `#F7F8FF` |
| Primary | Electric Violet | `#754CFF` |
| Digital | Cyan | `#36D6D0` |
| Energy | Acid Lime | `#C8F24A` |
| Warmth | Soft Orange | `#FF8A5B` |
| Support | Periwinkle | `#A8B5FF` |

### Typography

- **Display:** CoolveticaRound by LetUs.
- **Body/UI:** Plus Jakarta Sans.
- **Technical accent:** Chivo Mono for labels, steps, coordinates, project versions, and code-related microcopy.

This directly reuses the Let Us Design font family while assigning each typeface a clearer role.

### Image and Illustration Style

- Friendly diagrams, nodes, pathways, and simple isometric creative tools.
- Dark-violet project stages placed inside otherwise bright layouts.
- Close-up photography of a child explaining their project, not merely looking at a laptop.
- Gentle glow and translucent shapes used sparingly.
- Abstract “idea creatures” generated by combining code-like modules.

### Signature Device

**Idea Portal:** a rounded frame that transforms sketches into digital creations. In motion, objects enter as rough lines and emerge as colorful interactive elements.

### Sample Art Direction

> Bright friendly creative technology lab, children presenting colorful digital projects, soft violet and cyan interface overlays, rounded geometry, subtle grids, human and optimistic, premium but accessible, no neon cyberpunk atmosphere.

## Recommended Hybrid

Use **Creative Playground** as the core identity, borrow **Idea Notebook** for process storytelling, and reserve **Friendly Future Lab** elements for coding modules and institutional documents.

Recommended master palette:

| Token | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `create-ink` | `#121212` | Headlines and high-contrast text |
| `create-cream` | `#FFF9EC` | Primary background |
| `create-purple` | `#7C4DFF` | Brand anchor and primary CTA |
| `create-yellow` | `#FFD84D` | Highlights and moments of discovery |
| `create-lime` | `#B9F45D` | Progress and successful experiments |
| `create-pink` | `#FF6685` | Imagination and expressive accents |
| `create-blue` | `#55C7FF` | Coding, systems, and information |

Recommended typography:

- CoolveticaRound by LetUs for brand expression.
- Plus Jakarta Sans for clarity and trust.
- Chivo Mono for code and system details.
- Gloria Hallelujah only for human annotations.

## Logo Direction

Keep the words **Let Us** typographically related across the family. Give **Create** its own expressive behavior rather than creating an unrelated symbol.

Three explorations:

1. **Growing wordmark:** each letter in `CREATE` contains a subtle evolving shape, suggesting an idea becoming complete.
2. **Cursor wordmark:** a small cursor or spark appears after `Create`, implying the brand invites participation.
3. **Building-block wordmark:** one letter is assembled from two or three rounded modules and can animate or rearrange.

Recommended lockups:

```text
LET US CREATE
Create From Day One.
```

```text
LET US CREATE
A creative learning initiative by Let Us Design
```

Avoid puzzle-piece symbols, graduation caps, lightbulbs, brains, rockets, and literal `</>` code icons. They are generic and restrict the brand's future beyond coding.

## Photography Principles

### Show

- Children making choices, experimenting, and presenting.
- Hands, sketches, tools, prototypes, and work in progress.
- Facilitators at the child's eye level, asking or listening.
- Genuine concentration, surprise, and pride—not only smiles.
- The child's creation clearly visible whenever possible.

### Avoid

- Generic stock photos of children pointing at blank laptops.
- Staged high-fives as the primary visual language.
- Screens dominating every composition.
- Overly polished projects that feel adult-made.
- Visual stereotypes that coding is only for one gender or personality type.

Always obtain appropriate guardian consent for identifiable child photography and use a clear media-release process with partner institutions.

## Illustration System

Build a flexible set of **Idea Creatures**: friendly abstract characters made from circles, lines, rounded rectangles, cursors, and small pixels. Each character can represent a learning behavior rather than a school subject:

- **Wonder:** asks questions and carries a magnifying shape.
- **Try:** changes form through three iterations.
- **Oops:** celebrates unexpected results.
- **Make:** combines separate pieces into one creation.
- **Share:** opens a frame to present a finished project.

Keep faces minimal and expressions readable. Shapes should feel drawable by a child but composed with professional restraint.

## Layout Principles

- Prefer open, modular sections instead of dense card walls.
- Use a visible creative grid, dotted canvas, or notebook baseline.
- Let children's work break out of containers occasionally.
- Balance one bold playful moment with generous whitespace.
- Make interaction cues obvious; accessibility is part of good facilitation.
- Use rounded corners consistently, not on every object indiscriminately.

## Motion Principles

- Motion should demonstrate cause and effect.
- Let objects respond to input: click, drag, connect, transform.
- Reveal a journey from sketch to creation.
- Keep transitions short and purposeful.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and never make animation necessary to understand content.

## Accessibility Baseline

- Body text should meet WCAG AA contrast requirements.
- Never encode program level or status through color alone.
- Maintain a minimum 16px body size on the website.
- Use large tap targets and obvious focus states.
- Provide captions or transcripts for classroom videos.
- Use alt text to describe the learning activity or creation, not clothing or decorative context.
